15-Minute Warm-Up — train every row before the mock

Do one set right before a mock test. Every set runs in the same order, so a single 15-minute run covers the whole keyboard — like warming up every muscle before a match:

0–3 minTop-row lines
3–6 minHome-row lines
6–9 minBottom-row lines
9–12 minMeaningful words & sentences
12–15 minHard meaningless strings
⏱️ How to use: tap a set — it loads into the typing test with the timer set to 15 minutes. Type it in order, accuracy first. The meaningless strings are deliberate: with no meaning to guess from, your fingers learn the true movements — that is what makes the mock feel easy afterwards.

Part 0 — Start here: fingers, home position, warm-up

Home position: left fingers rest on A S D F, right fingers on J K L ; — feel the bumps on F and J. Thumbs share the space bar. Never look at the keyboard; return to home after every reach.

Practice Sessions — sentences & full exam paragraphs

Longer, exam-style typing, grouped by difficulty. Work Easy → Medium → Hard, and finish every practice day with the ⭐ 10-minute simulation at exam duration — that is the session that predicts your real marks.

🎯 Session rule: pick the paragraph, set the duration above it in the Typing Test (Easy 2–5 min · Medium 5 min · Hard / Simulation 10 min — EXAM), then type paper-to-screen style in 3–4 word chunks.

Part 8 — How your marks are actually calculated

Gross WPM = everything you typed ÷ 5 characters ÷ minutes. Net WPM = gross speed after errors are deducted — and net WPM is the only number Delhi Police pays marks for. Because HCM counts no half mistakes, one wrong letter, one missing capital, one wrong punctuation mark or one extra space costs exactly as much as a completely wrong word.

Worked example: you type 55 WPM gross but make errors on 6% of words. Those errors drag net speed to roughly 48–49 — you fall from the 25-mark band to 21. Type at 52 gross with almost no errors and your net stays above 50: full 25 marks. That is why this whole course is accuracy-first.

Exam technique — paper to screen

  • Read in chunks of 3–4 words from the paper, then type the chunk without looking back mid-chunk.
  • Keep eyes moving between paper and screen at phrase boundaries, not word by word.
  • First 30 seconds deliberately at 80% speed — a clean start settles rhythm for the full 10 minutes.
  • If you lose your place, find the last completed word on the paper — never re-type a line.
  • Do not stop to admire or mourn an error. In the hall, keep moving; accuracy habits built here do the rest.

Exam-hall checklist — for you, Ekta

  • Admit card + original photo ID + photocopies ready the night before
  • Reach the centre 45–60 minutes early; report before gate closure
  • Check your allotted keyboard early — report stiff or faulty keys immediately
  • Adjust chair height: forearms level, feet flat, screen at eye level
  • During the demo / practice time, test Space, Backspace and Shift keys
  • First 30 seconds at 80% speed — settle, then build rhythm
  • Type in 3–4 word chunks from the paper; never re-type a line
  • Capitals with the opposite hand's Shift — every single time
  • An error happens: keep moving, Ekta. Net speed rewards the full 10 minutes — and you have trained exactly for this
